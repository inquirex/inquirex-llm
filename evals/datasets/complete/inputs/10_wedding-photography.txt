I've been putting off writing this because every time I try, it turns into an essay about my family, so apologies in advance. Alan and I met at a wildfire lookout tower eight years ago, and we decided early that we didn't want to do the courthouse-and-a-dinner thing his brother did. We wanted the whole sprawling, slightly chaotic day with everyone we love, which, once my mother finishes adding cousins I have never met, looks like a hundred and forty people, give or take a handful.

The date is Sunday, October 17, 2027, which we picked because the maples in Vermont should be at their absolute peak. We live in Chicago, so everyone, ourselves included and presumably you, will be flying or driving out. I keep picturing how it all unfolds: the morning in the old inn with my sisters, the light coming through those wavy glass windows while my hair is being done around noon; then the ceremony at the white chapel down the road; then the walk across the covered bridge to the barn for dinner and dancing, ending with a sparkler send-off around ten at night. So yes, I want you there for all of it, from the first bobby pin to the last sparkler, and I suppose that's three places if you're counting.

I've learned that stills alone won't satisfy my grandmother, who wants to hear the vows again, so we'd like a filmmaker as well, ideally a short highlight film alongside the photographs. My uncle, who flies drones as a hobby and has strong opinions, insists we have aerial shots of the foliage; I'd rather a professional did that than him. I'd love a real printed album, the heavy linen kind you pass down, not just a gallery link. And because Alan's side and my side will be getting ready in different buildings, I assume we'd need a second photographer so his morning isn't a blank. I don't need anything fancier than that. Is any of this possible?
